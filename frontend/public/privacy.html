Logjam — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-09-28

Logjam is built as a private tool. Privacy is a design constraint, not a feature, because exposing place locations causes real environmental harm and can compromise rescue and access agreements. This policy explains what we store, why, and what we deliberately do not do. It covers both ways of using Logjam: Logjam Web in your browser and Logjam GPS, the phone app.

Logjam is intended for users in Australia. It is a personal, non-commercial project operated by an individual and is not currently required to comply with the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth). We nonetheless handle personal information in accordance with the Australian Privacy Principles as a voluntary standard.

What we store

  • Account data: username, email, sign-in credentials (via AWS Cognito), theme and UI preferences, storage and monthly usage, and which version of these terms and this policy you agreed to and when.
  • Place data: name, coordinates, grades, notes, attributes, place types, the custom fields you define, links between places, and any media you upload.
  • Routes and tracks: routes you draw or import, and GPS tracks you record in Logjam GPS. A finished recording is uploaded to your account as a GPX file while you are signed in.
  • Trip logs: dates, notes, custom fields, and media attached to your own trips.
  • Friends and sharing: your friends list, what you have shared with whom, and files you send to friends.
  • Topo and GeoPDF jobs: the area each job covers, the LiDAR inputs you upload, and the map tiles and PDFs produced from them.
  • Devices: for each Logjam GPS install that allows notifications, a push token (an address for sending notifications to that phone) and its platform.
  • Operational logs: request logs, error logs and worker job state. Our application scrubs these so place names and coordinates do not appear in plain text.
  • Access and audit logs: records of requests to our servers and storage, including your IP address and, for web-server logs, your browser or device type and the address requested. See “Retention” for how long each is kept.

Logjam GPS on your phone

Logjam GPS keeps a copy of your data on the phone so it works without signal, along with any map regions you download for offline use. Your location is used on the phone to show where you are and to record tracks you start; it is not sent to us except as places, routes and tracks you save. You can use Logjam GPS without an account: that data stays on the phone and is not sent to us until you sign in and link it to an account.

What we do not do

  • No third-party analytics or advertising. The only telemetry is crash reporting in Logjam GPS, which is off until you turn it on (see “Crash reports” below). Map tiles are fetched from third-party providers so the map can render — see “Third-party map services” below for exactly what they receive.
  • No public or unauthenticated endpoints on user data — every API route that serves user data requires you to be signed in.
  • No default public sharing. Sharing is explicit, one item at a time, between signed-in users you are friends with.
  • No selling, renting, or monetising your data.

How sharing works

You can share only with Logjam users you are friends with (one of you sends a friend request and the other accepts it), and only what you choose, one item at a time.

When you share a place with a friend, they can see the place record including its name, coordinates, place-level notes and place-level media, and the route linked to it. Per-trip notes, per-trip media, and your trip-log history remain private to you. You can also share a route, a topo map or a GeoPDF on its own.

While something is shared with someone, they can copy it into their own account or export it to a file on their device. Revoking a share stops future access through Logjam, but it does not retract copies or exports the recipient has already made.

Sending a copy of a file (an imported GPX, KML or GeoJSON file, or a recorded track) is different from sharing: a friend who accepts it gets their own copy, which you cannot take back. The file is held for them for 7 days and then deleted from our storage.

Where data is hosted

Logjam runs on AWS in the ap-southeast-2 (Sydney) region. Photos, topo input, and rendered tiles are stored in S3. The database is RDS PostgreSQL. Authentication is handled by Amazon Cognito within the same Australian region. Deleting a stored file is final: our storage keeps no earlier versions to restore. Email, push notifications, app updates and crash reports go through providers outside Australia (see “Overseas disclosure” below).

Overseas disclosure

Some data leaves Australia. All of Logjam's outbound email is delivered through Resend, whose email is processed in the United States. This covers two kinds of message:

  • Account emails: the codes that verify your email address, reset your password, or confirm an email change (and, for accounts created by invitation, a temporary password). Each contains your email address and a single time-limited code.
  • Notification emails: telling you a topo job, export, or GeoPDF you started has finished. Each contains your email address and a job reference.

Neither kind of email contains place names or coordinates. Sending them is a cross-border disclosure of your email address, and — for account emails — of a short-lived authentication code.

Logjam GPS also relies on these services outside Australia:

  • Push notifications: sent through Expo (United States), which passes them to Google Firebase Cloud Messaging for delivery to your phone. They receive the phone’s push token and each notification, which carries a fixed title (such as “A place was shared with you”), its type and reference IDs — never a place name, coordinates, username or any text you wrote. Logjam GPS registers for notifications only if you allow them.
  • App updates: when it starts, Logjam GPS asks Expo (u.expo.dev, United States) whether a newer version of the app is available. Expo receives your IP address, the installed app version and platform, and a random identifier for the install — not your account or any of your data.
  • Crash reports (only if you turn them on): sent to Sentry (United States). See “Crash reports” below.

We take reasonable steps to ensure overseas recipients handle your information consistently with the Australian Privacy Principles.

The service is delivered through Amazon CloudFront, a global content-delivery network. Authenticated requests may transit CloudFront edge locations outside Australia while in transit, but they are not cached there, and all of your data is stored and processed in Australia (ap-southeast-2), apart from what this section and “Third-party map services” describe.

Crash reports

Logjam GPS asks once whether to send crash reports, and they stay off unless you say yes. You can change your answer at any time in Settings → Privacy and security. Logjam Web and our servers do not send crash reports to anyone.

A crash report goes to Sentry (Functional Software, Inc., United States). It contains the error and where in the app it happened, recent app activity leading up to it, and technical details such as the app version, phone model and operating system. It does not contain your account identity. Before a report leaves your phone, Logjam GPS removes web addresses, coordinate pairs and map-tile references from its text, and removes any field named for a location, a name, notes, field values or tags. Like any server, Sentry sees your phone’s IP address when a report arrives.

Operator access

We will not pretend otherwise: Logjam is run by an operator who holds administrative access to the servers and can, technically, read the data stored in them. This is not end-to-end encryption, and losing your password does not make your data unreadable to us.

What we do instead is make that access accountable. Operator access to the database and storage is recorded in tamper-evident, write-once audit logs that the operator cannot delete, and the application itself connects with a restricted, non-administrative database account so that operator access stands out from ordinary app traffic. The application’s own routine reads are not individually logged (they are automatic, high-volume, and carry no operator-access signal), but every operator read and every database connection — which account connected and from where — is logged, so administrative access cannot quietly hide among normal app activity. We keep as little as possible: no third-party analytics, crash reports only if you turn them on, short retention on logs and exported files, and you can export or permanently delete your data yourself at any time.

To be clear, this describes what is technically possible, not what we do. We will not read, use, share, or sell your data for personal gain or curiosity — only to operate, maintain, and debug the service on your behalf. The controls above exist to keep that commitment accountable, rather than something you simply have to take on faith.

The full commitment — including the honest limits of this approach and how to verify the logging configuration in our source repository — is in our Data-Access Policy.

Third-party map services

Rendering maps requires fetching tiles and map data from third-party providers. None of these requests carry your account identity, but most carry location information about the map areas involved:

  • Basemap tiles: when you pan or zoom the map in Logjam Web or Logjam GPS, or download a region for offline use in Logjam GPS, your browser or phone requests tiles directly from the operator of the selected base layer — OpenTopoMap, CyclOSM (openstreetmap.fr), or NSW Spatial Services / DCS SIX Maps (maps.six.nsw.gov.au — the topographic, base, and imagery layers). The operator receives your IP address and the tile coordinates of the areas you view, which at high zoom indicate the locations you are looking at. The default vector basemap is served by Logjam itself.
  • Terrain and elevation: the 3D terrain in Logjam Web, and offline region downloads in Logjam GPS, fetch elevation tiles from the AWS Open Data terrain tile set (s3.amazonaws.com, hosted in the United States). As with basemap tiles, the host receives your IP address and the tile coordinates of the area. Elevation profiles are normally worked out on our servers, which fetch the same tiles without your IP address; Logjam GPS fetches them itself only when our servers can’t answer or you are using it without an account.
  • Map fonts and symbols: Logjam Web loads the vector basemap’s fonts and icons from Protomaps’ assets on GitHub Pages (protomaps.github.io, United States), and Logjam GPS does so on first launch until it has its own copy. These requests carry your IP address but no location.
  • Place search: when you search the map for a place name in Logjam Web or Logjam GPS, the text you type is sent to the OpenStreetMap Foundation’s Nominatim service (nominatim.openstreetmap.org) along with your IP address. Nothing else about you or your places is sent.
  • Server-side GeoPDF rendering: when you generate a GeoPDF, our servers fetch the same providers’ tiles for the export area. The providers see our server’s requests — your IP address and account are not sent.
  • Topo jobs: our rendering worker requests OpenStreetMap data for the job’s footprint bounding box from public Overpass API mirrors (overpass-api.de, overpass.kumi.systems, overpass.private.coffee). No account identifiers are sent.

These providers operate under their own privacy policies and are not given any way to associate the requests with your Logjam account.

Email

We email you only for:

  • Account verification, password reset and email-change codes.
  • Notification when a topo job, export or GeoPDF you started has finished.

No marketing email is ever sent.

Your controls

  • Export & access: the Account panel in Logjam Web has a "Download my data" button that produces a machine-readable JSON copy of the account data we hold about you — your account details, places, place types, custom fields, links, routes, trip logs, media metadata, friends, shares, sent and received files, topo and GeoPDF jobs and templates (including their map footprints), notifications, and registered devices. No request to us is required; the export is generated on demand. (Media file contents are not included in the JSON; the metadata identifies each item. Topo tiles and PDFs are not included; the job records identify them. Access and audit logs are not included; ask us if you want what they hold about you.)
  • Delete: "Delete account" (the Account panel in Logjam Web, or Account in Logjam GPS) permanently removes your sign-in and every account record we hold for you, including places, routes, trip logs, media, friendships, shares, and notifications. Log records are not removed by account deletion; they expire as described under “Retention”.
  • Correct: you can edit your username, email, place records, and trip logs at any time, and can ask us to correct any other personal information that is inaccurate.

Data breaches

If a data breach occurs that is likely to result in serious harm, we will assess it and notify affected users and, where appropriate, the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC), consistent with the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme.

Complaints

If you have a privacy concern, contact us first at the address below and we will respond. If you are not satisfied with our response, you may also seek guidance from the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC) at oaic.gov.au.

Retention

Data is retained until you delete it or the account, with these exceptions, which expire automatically:

  • Files sent to friends as a copy: 7 days.
  • Topo export and GeoPDF files: 7 days after they are produced (the job records stay until you delete them).

Logs are kept after the record they relate to is removed, including after you delete your account, and expire automatically:

  • API request and web-server logs (which include your IP address and browser or device type): 7 days.
  • Logs from the service that sends account emails: 14 days.
  • Storage access logs, which record the IP address that fetched or uploaded each file and are used to meter monthly download limits: 30 days.
  • Rendering-worker job logs: 90 days.
  • The operator-access audit logs described under “Operator access”: 365 days, in storage that cannot be altered or deleted before then. Besides operator activity, they record each upload and download of your media and topo files, including the IP address and the file’s storage path, which contains your account’s internal ID.

Our application scrubs its operational logs so place names and coordinates do not appear in plain text.

Children

Logjam is not directed at anyone under 18, and you must not create an account for a person under 18.
