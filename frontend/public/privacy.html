Logjam — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-06-21

Logjam is built as a private tool. Privacy is a design constraint, not a feature, because exposing canyon locations causes real environmental harm and can compromise rescue and access agreements. This policy explains what we store, why, and what we deliberately do not do.

Logjam is intended for users in Australia. We handle personal information in line with the Australian Privacy Principles under the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth).

What we store

  • Account data: username, email, sign-in credentials (via AWS Cognito), theme and UI preferences, storage usage.
  • Canyon data: name, coordinates, grades, notes, attributes, and any media you upload.
  • Trip logs: dates, notes, custom fields, and media attached to your own trips.
  • Sharing: which of your friends each canyon is shared with.
  • Topo jobs: LiDAR inputs you upload and the rendered map tiles produced from them.
  • Operational logs: request error logs and worker job state. These are scrubbed so canyon names and coordinates do not appear in plain text.

What we do not do

  • No third-party analytics, advertising, or telemetry leaving your account. (Map tiles are fetched from third-party providers so the map can render — see “Third-party map services” below for exactly what they receive.)
  • No public or unauthenticated endpoints on user data — every API route requires authentication.
  • No default public sharing. Sharing is explicit, per-canyon, between authenticated users you have a friendship with.
  • No selling, renting, or monetising your data.

How sharing works

When you share a canyon with another Logjam user, that user can see the canyon record including its name, coordinates, canyon-level notes and canyon-level media. Per-trip notes, per-trip media, and your trip-log history remain private to you.

While a canyon is shared with someone, they can copy it into their own account or export it to a file on their device. Revoking a share stops future access through Logjam, but it does not retract copies or exports the recipient has already made.

Where data is hosted

Logjam runs on AWS in the ap-southeast-2 (Sydney) region. Photos, topo input, and rendered tiles are stored in S3. The database is RDS PostgreSQL. Transactional email is sent via Resend. Authentication is Amazon Cognito.

Operator access

We will not pretend otherwise: Logjam is run by an operator who holds administrative access to the servers and can, technically, read the data stored in them. This is not end-to-end encryption, and losing your password does not make your data unreadable to us.

What we do instead is make that access accountable. Operator access to the database and storage is recorded in tamper-evident, write-once audit logs that the operator cannot delete, and the application itself connects with a restricted, non-administrative database account so that operator access stands out from ordinary app traffic. The application’s own routine reads are not individually logged (they are automatic, high-volume, and carry no operator-access signal), but every operator read and every database connection — which account connected and from where — is logged, so administrative access cannot quietly hide among normal app activity. We keep as little as possible: no third-party analytics or telemetry, short retention on topo data, and you can export or permanently delete your data yourself at any time.

To be clear, this describes what is technically possible, not what we do. We will not read, use, share, or sell your data for personal gain or curiosity — only to operate, maintain, and debug the service on your behalf. The controls above exist to keep that commitment accountable, rather than something you simply have to take on faith.

The full commitment — including the honest limits of this approach and how to verify the logging configuration in our source repository — is in our Data-Access Policy.

Third-party map services

Rendering maps requires fetching tiles and map data from third-party providers. None of these requests carry your account identity, but they do carry location information about the map areas involved:

  • Basemap tiles in your browser: when you pan or zoom the map, your browser requests tiles directly from the operator of the selected base layer — the OpenStreetMap Foundation (tile.openstreetmap.org), OpenTopoMap, CyclOSM (openstreetmap.fr), or NSW Spatial Services / DCS SIX Maps (maps.six.nsw.gov.au — the topographic, base, and imagery layers). The operator receives your IP address and the tile coordinates of the areas you view, which at high zoom indicate the locations you are looking at.
  • Server-side GeoPDF rendering: when you generate a GeoPDF, our servers fetch the same providers’ tiles for the export area. The providers see our server’s requests — your IP address and account are not sent.
  • Topo jobs: our rendering worker requests OpenStreetMap data for the job’s footprint bounding box from public Overpass API mirrors (overpass-api.de, overpass.kumi.systems, overpass.private.coffee). No account identifiers are sent.

These providers operate under their own privacy policies and are not given any way to associate the requests with your Logjam account.

Email

We email you only for:

  • Account verification and password reset codes.
  • Notification when a topo job you started has finished.

No marketing email is ever sent.

Your controls

  • Export & access: the Account panel has a "Download my data" button that produces a complete machine-readable JSON copy of the personal information we hold about you — canyons, trip logs, GeoPDF and topo templates, topo job records (including their map footprints), shares, notifications, and media metadata. No request to us is required; the export is generated on demand in the app. (Media file contents are not included in the JSON; the metadata identifies each item. Topo tile files are not included; the job records identify them.)
  • Delete: the Account panel has a "Delete account" button that permanently removes every record we hold for you, including canyons, trip logs, media, friendships, shares, and notifications.
  • Correct: you can edit your username, email, canyon records, and trip logs at any time, and can ask us to correct any other personal information that is inaccurate.

Data breaches

If a data breach occurs that is likely to result in serious harm, we will assess it and notify both the affected users and the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC) as required under the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme.

Complaints

If you have a privacy concern, contact us first at the address below and we will respond. If you are not satisfied with our response, you can lodge a complaint with the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC) at oaic.gov.au.

Retention

Data is retained until you delete it or the account. Operational logs are retained in AWS CloudWatch after the originating record is removed: API request logs expire automatically within 7 days; rendering-worker job logs have a 90-day automatic expiry. All operational logs are scrubbed so canyon names and coordinates do not appear in plain text.

Children

Logjam is not directed at children under 16. Do not create an account for a minor.

Changes to this policy

When this policy materially changes, the "Last updated" date above will change and you will be asked to re-consent on next sign-in.
